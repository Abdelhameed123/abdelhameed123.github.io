🎮 nvidia-macos-driver - Unlock Metal Graphics on Your Mac

A straightforward page for anyone who wants to get their NVIDIA GeForce card working smoothly with Metal on macOS Sequoia. No programming knowledge required.

⬇️ Download nvidia-macos-driver

🚀 Getting Started

This project is a Metal driver for NVIDIA GeForce graphics cards running on macOS Sequoia. In plain terms: your Mac and your NVIDIA graphics card normally have trouble talking to each other. This driver acts as a translator, so the graphics card can be used properly for drawing windows, animations, video playback, and other visual work.

You do not need to understand how a driver works in order to use this one. You only need to visit the download page, get the application, and then follow the simple guidance below. Everything on this page is written for a regular user who simply wants the graphics card to behave the way it should.

🧰 What You Need Before You Begin

Having these things ready makes the whole process much smoother:

  • A Mac running macOS Sequoia.
  • A desktop Mac or a Mac with an NVIDIA GeForce graphics card installed in it.

  • About fifteen minutes of free time, so you are not rushing.

✨ What This Driver Does for You

nvidia-macos-driver is built around Metal, the graphics technology macOS uses for nearly everything visual. Once the driver is in place, your NVIDIA GeForce card can take part in that work instead of sitting idle.

  • Smoother everyday graphics. Window animations, scrolling, and switching between apps feel more fluid.
  • Metal support. Apps that rely on Metal gain access to the graphics card's processing power.
  • Vulkan compatibility. Tools and games that use Vulkan can benefit from the additional rendering path.
  • OpenCore friendly. Designed to sit alongside an OpenCore-based setup without fighting it.
  • Installed as a kext. The driver loads at system level, which is what lets it communicate with the hardware directly.
  • Built for Sequoia. Targeted at macOS Sequoia rather than older macOS releases.

You do not need to configure any of this. Installing the driver is the whole job — the rest happens automatically in the background once the Mac restarts.
